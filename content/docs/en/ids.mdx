# Canonical eulogy IDs

Every eulogy on this site has an identifier, such as `mr:0102-telesphorus`. This page explains why, and
the rules that form one.

<Banner>All identifiers are drafts pending review by a committee. The `mr:` prefix and the choice of the
2004 edition as the anchor may still change; if they do, every identifier will be rewritten mechanically.</Banner>

## Why identifiers

The same eulogy appears in many editions and languages, worded differently, numbered differently, and
sometimes on another day. To say "this eulogy" across all of them, it needs a name that belongs to none
of them. An identifier lets one:

- cite a eulogy without quoting it, in any language;
- set two editions side by side, eulogy by eulogy;
- attach data to it: its subject, its places, its notes;
- keep that name when a text is corrected or a new edition appears.

## The form: mr:MMDD-slug

An identifier has two parts after the prefix `mr:`:

- **`MMDD`**, the month and day on which the edition prints the eulogy: `0102` is 2 January. For the
  eulogies of the current book this is the day of the *editio typica altera* of 2004.
- **The slug**, the name of the eulogy's first-named subject in the Latin nominative, in lowercase
  ASCII, with no honorific (*sanctus*, *beatus*): `telesphorus`, `basilius-magnus-et-gregorius-nazianzenus`.

The registry holds 4,640 current identifiers, for the eulogies of the 2004 edition, and 1,616
deprecated ones (see [Deprecated IDs](#deprecated-ids)).

## What is identity, and what is not

- **The day is part of the identity.** A eulogy that an edition prints on another day has its own
  identifier there; the two are linked as the same eulogy (`same_eulogy`), never merged.
- **Entry number, asterisk and unnumbered status are not.** They belong to each edition: the 2004
  Latin and the Italian edition may number a day differently, or asterisk different entries, and the
  identifier stays the same.
- **The text is not.** An identifier names the eulogy, not its wording, so it survives corrections and
  translations.
- **Identifiers are unique**, and within a day no two share a slug.

## Building the slug

The slug is the Latin nominative of the first-named subject, folded to plain letters:

- the genitive of the text turned back to the nominative: *sancti Basilii* → `basilius`;
- lowercase, words joined by hyphens, diacritics folded (*æ* → `ae`, *ł* → `l`), classical *i* for *j*:
  `ioannes`, not `joannes`;
- no honorific, and no numbers: same-day namesakes are told apart by a place or an epithet
  (`mr:0408-dionysius-corinthi`), never by `-2`;
- one name form, never a mix of two; for a consecrated person, the secular name (see below);
- papal ordinals in roman numerals (`pius-v`); other regnal ordinals as Latin words (`ludovicus-nonus`);
- an epithet the eulogy gives with *cognomento* is kept: `petrus-chrysologus`.

### Religious and secular names

A religious often has two names: the one given at baptism and the one taken in religion. The 2004 Latin
usually prints both, the secular name in parentheses: *sancti Raphaélis a Sancto Ioseph (Ioséphi)
Kalinowski*. The slug takes the **secular name and surname**, `mr:1115-iosephus-kalinowski`, and the
religious name, Raphael a Sancto Ioseph, is kept as another name of the person. The secular name is
preferred because:

- it is less likely to **collide**: religious names repeat across congregations and centuries (three
  saints and blesseds are *Maria a Iesu*, three *Maria ab Incarnatione*);
- it is generally **shorter**, with no title or place (*a Sancto Ioseph*, *ab Immaculata Conceptione*);
- it is less **obscure**: records, biographies and Wikidata use the baptismal name and surname.

The exception is a religious name so widely used that the saint is known by it, like Pio of Pietrelcina
(`mr:0923-pius-de-pietrelcina`): then the religious name makes the slug, and the secular name is the
other name. Never a mix of the two: not `raphael-kalinowski`. These identifiers are being renamed after
review.

### Several subjects and groups

| Subjects | Slug | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Two | both, joined by `-et-` | `mr:0927-cosmas-et-damianus`, `mr:0622-ioannes-fisher-et-thomas-more` |
| Three or more, or "and companions" | the first, then `-et-socii` | `mr:0206-paulus-miki-et-socii` |
| An anonymous group | `[number-]class-` and the place in the genitive | `mr:0309-quadraginta-milites-sebastes` |
| A group known by a name | `martyres-` and that name | `mr:0717-martyres-scillitani` |

### Feasts and titled saints

Feasts of the Lord, of Mary and other celebrations take a set phrase, without *sancti*/*sanctae*, except
for the name of a church, the Holy Cross, All Saints and *beatae Mariae*. Prophets keep `-propheta`, the
Twelve `-apostolus` (`mr:0629-petrus-et-paulus-apostoli`), the evangelists `-evangelista`.

### No bare names

A single name alone, like `dominicus`, says little: many saints share it. So a single-name slug takes a
discriminator, the first of these that applies:

1. the **surname**, when the saint is commonly known by it;
2. the **place**, in the Latin genitive: a bishop's see, an abbot's monastery, otherwise the place of
   death (Dominic, who died at Bologna, is drafted as `dominicus-bononiae`);
3. an **epithet**, when there is no surname and no place: `-papa` for a pope with no ordinal, a biblical
   title, or a byname the eulogy gives.

The same saint carries the same discriminator on every day. These discriminators are being drafted for
the whole registry and are under review.

### Consistency

Four more rules keep identifiers consistent. The registry checks the first, second and fourth
with `scripts/lint_ids.py` before an identifier is minted or renamed:

- **One saint, one slug.** Every eulogy of one person (one Wikidata item) names the person alike, on
  every day and in every edition: Augustine of Canterbury is not `augustinus-cantuariae` on 26 May
  and `augustinus-cantuariensis` on 27 May. Tools depend on it: a eulogy that says *cuius memoria
  cras agitur* ("whose memorial is tomorrow") is linked to the next day's eulogy by its identifier.
- **The printed diphthong is kept.** A place printed with *æ* or *œ* keeps it as `ae` or `oe`:
  *Córdubæ* gives `cordubae`, never `cordube`.
- **The place keeps its own form**, from the place the text prints: *Naziánzi* gives `nonna-nazianzi`,
  not the masculine adjective `nazianzenus` of another saint.
- **No identifier is reused**, not even a deprecated one: a new identifier that would equal an
  existing one, current or deprecated, takes another form.

## Deprecated IDs

Older editions contain eulogies that the 2004 book does not print, or prints on another day. Each has
its own identifier, marked **deprecated**, with the edition it is attested in (`attested_in`) and, where
there is one, a link to the same eulogy elsewhere. For example, `mr:0102-martinianus` is the eulogy of
Martinian that the 1749 edition prints on 2 January; the 2004 edition prints it on 29 December, as
`mr:1229-martinianus`. Deprecated identifiers follow the same rules and are never reused.

## Recognizability

The aim of all these rules is that a reader can recognize the eulogy from its identifier: who it is, and
where needed, which one. Whether an identifier achieves that is a judgement that needs readers;
see [How to contribute](/docs/contributing).
