# The Martyrology and the particular calendars

The General Roman Calendar lists the celebrations kept by the whole Roman Rite. Dioceses, nations
and religious families keep their own saints as well, in their proper calendars. The Roman
Martyrology stands between the two: it contains every saint of the General Calendar, and many,
though not all, of the saints of the particular calendars. This page explains how the book and those
calendars fit together. The *Praenotanda* rely throughout on the Instruction *Calendaria
particularia* of the Sacred Congregation for Divine Worship (24 June 1970).

## The General Calendar and the particular calendars

The martyrology lists the saints inscribed in the Roman Calendar, whose importance is universal
across the Roman Rite, and also many of those commended to a particular Church or religious family
and commemorated there at some liturgical rank <Cite n="29" />.

Every diocese and every religious family should have its own proper calendar, and each conference of
bishops draws up the proper calendar of its nation, or, with other conferences, of a wider region.
All these calendars must agree with the Roman Martyrology, and all are approved or confirmed by the
Apostolic See <Cite n="32" />.

## Saints and blessed

A **saint** inscribed in the martyrology on a given day may be celebrated on that day, in the Mass
and the Office, wherever an optional memorial is allowed <Cite n="30" />.

A **blessed** may be celebrated only in the diocese, nation, region or religious family to which the
Apostolic See has granted that cult <Cite n="31" />. The asterisk beside an entry number marks this
particular or local standing; see [Asterisks](/docs/reading-a-day#asterisks).

## When the dies natalis is impeded

A saint's day in the martyrology is normally their *dies natalis*, the day of their death and birth
into heaven. If that day is impeded every year by a celebration of higher rank, the proper calendar
may keep the saint on the nearest free day, or on another day connected with them: the finding,
elevation or translation of their relics, or, less fittingly, the day of their canonization or
beatification. The eulogy is then read with one of the formulas for a transferred memorial
<Cite n="33" />; see [Transferred memorials](/docs/using#transferred-memorials).

## Titulars of churches

Any saint inscribed in the Roman Martyrology may be chosen as the titular of a church. A blessed may
be chosen only with an indult of the Apostolic See, unless that blessed is already lawfully inscribed
in the proper calendar of the diocese or nation <Cite n="34" />.

## The Propria of the Martyrology

A diocese, nation or religious family may draw up a **Proper of the Martyrology** (*Proprium* or
*Appendix Martyrologii*). It lists the saints and blessed of its proper calendar who are absent from
the Roman Martyrology, who are kept there on another day or at another rank, or whose eulogy it has
seemed right to give at a little more length. Such a Proper is sent to the Congregation for Divine
Worship for review and approval or confirmation <Cite n="38" />.

The *Praenotanda* also say how these eulogies are to be written. They are not to imitate the genre of
the "Lives" or "Legends"; as far as possible they show the paschal victory of Christ in his servants
and the particular grace given to each. Historical truth is to be kept strictly, with no homiletic
or devotional matter, and a eulogy should not exceed about **forty words** <Cite n="39" />.

## The editions of the conferences

The conferences of bishops prepare translations of the martyrology into modern languages, keeping
the text whole and faithful while respecting its literary genre <Cite n="40" />.

In a conference's edition, the eulogies proper to a whole nation or region, by concession of the Holy
See, come first on their day, right after the eulogies of the celebrations of the General Calendar,
and are printed in the same type. Eulogies proper to a territory or a diocese always go into a
separate appendix. Every such edition is approved by the conference and reviewed by the Apostolic
See; the same holds, with the necessary changes, for a religious family <Cite n="41" />.

Finally, a translation of the Roman Martyrology, which must be complete, is not the same as a
partial collection drawn from it for pastoral use; such collections are not meant for liturgical use
<Cite n="42" />.
