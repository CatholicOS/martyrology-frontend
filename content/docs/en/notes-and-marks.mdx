# Notes, misprints and errata

A page in the reader shows two kinds of things: what the edition itself prints, and what the curators
of this site add. The site keeps them visibly apart, so a reader always knows which is which.

## What the edition prints

Some editions print notes of their own. The 1630 edition is the richest: under each day's eulogies come
Baronius's **footnotes** (*Notationes*), on the sources of each eulogy, keyed to marks in the text as
they are printed; and in the margin, small **marginal notes** refer to the places in his *Annales
Ecclesiastici* where each saint is discussed. The site shows them as printed, in the edition's own
language, at the foot of the page and beside the text. See, for example,
[1 January in the 1630 edition](/read/martyrologium_romanum_1630/01/01).

## The edition's own errata

Old books often end with a list of errata: corrections the printer or the editor made after the pages
were printed. Where an edition's errata list corrects a eulogy, the site shows the correction right
after the words it concerns, in brown, as **[Errata: …]**. Hovering over it, or focusing it with the
keyboard, shows the erratum as printed and where it is found. For example, on
[16 January in the 1630 edition](/read/martyrologium_romanum_1630/01/16) the text prints *Berradi*, and
the errata correct it to *Berardi*.

## Misprints found by the curators

Some misprints are not corrected by the edition itself. When the curators have verified one against the
printed page, the site shows it, in grey, as **[sic! expected: …]**: *sic!* means the word is printed
so, and the expected reading follows. These are the site's findings, not the edition's: they are distinct
from its errata. Each is a single word, or a phrase of up to three words, and each is recorded in the open
registry (see [Data and sources](/docs/en/data)). For example, a misprint in the 2004 Latin edition is
marked on [12 June](/read/martyrologium_romanum_2004/06/12).

## Curator notes

Some things need more than a word. A **red dagger** † in the text marks a curator note, given in
English at the foot of the page, under a short rule: a second note on the same page is marked ††, and so
on. Daggers are used, rather than asterisks or numbers, so that they are never mistaken for the edition's
own marks. A note may record an error in the source, explain a decision about a eulogy's identity, or
point to the same eulogy on another day; the identifiers it names link to their eulogies. For example, a
note on [1 January in the Italian edition](/read/martyrologium_romanum_2004_it_IT/01/01) records a place
translated wrongly.

## The notes page of an edition

Every edition has a page that gathers all of these for the whole year: its footnotes, the curators'
notes, the misprints and the errata, each with its eulogy and a link to its day. It can be filtered by
kind. Open it from the reader, or at `/read/<edition>/notes`, for example
[the notes of the 1630 edition](/read/martyrologium_romanum_1630/notes).
