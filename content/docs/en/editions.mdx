# The editions on this site

The [home page](/) shows the editions of the Roman Martyrology as books on a shelf, newest to oldest.
Open one to read today's page, then turn the pages day by day. This page says what each edition is.

## Kinds of edition

- An **editio typica** is the official Latin text, the reference for every other edition. A revised
  reprint is an *editio typica recognita*; a second typical edition is an *editio typica altera*.
- An **editio vernacula** is a translation approved by a conference of bishops and confirmed by the
  Apostolic See, which becomes the official text for liturgical use in that language.
- An **unofficial translation** is a translation with no such approval. It is useful for reading, but it
  is not a liturgical book.

## The editions

| 2004 | Roman Martyrology | English | unofficial translation |
| 1914 | The Roman Martyrology (Baltimore, 1916) | English | unofficial translation |
| 1749 | *Martyrologium Romanum* of Benedict XIV | Latin | editio typica recognita |
| 1630 | *Martyrologium Romanum* of Urban VIII, with Baronius's notes | Latin | editio typica recognita |

**The 2004 Latin edition** is the edition in force: the *editio typica altera* promulgated on 29 June
2004 (see [History](/docs/history#the-editio-typica-altera-of-2004)). Its days give this site's
identifiers their dates (see [Canonical eulogy IDs](/docs/ids)). It prints the lunar table.

**The Italian edition** is the translation of the Italian Episcopal Conference, confirmed in 2006, the
official text for liturgical use in Italian. It prints the lunar table too, and its own Praenotanda
(*Premesse*). Where it differs from the Latin print, in its asterisks, the order of a day or an error of
translation, the site says so in a note.

**The 2004 English translation** is unofficial; no approved English edition of the 2004 text exists.

**The 1914 English translation** renders the Latin edition printed at Rome in 1914, and was published in
Baltimore in 1916 with the imprimatur of Cardinal Gibbons.

**The 1749 edition** is the edition of Benedict XIV, in a modern retyping of a 1913 printing; it
therefore includes the saints added after 1749, up to the beginning of the twentieth century.

**The 1630 edition** is the edition printed at the Vatican under Urban VIII, with Baronius's
*Notationes* at the foot of each page and, in the margin, references to his *Annales*. It is
transcribed from the printed book and proofread against the page images. It prints the lunar table.

Some editions are listed on the shelf but cannot be opened yet: the first edition of 1584, the Latin
edition of 1914 and the editio typica of 2001.

## Who can read the texts

The older editions are in the public domain. The 2004 texts are under copyright; the site currently
shows them to every reader, and may restrict them to signed-in readers in the future. The identifiers,
the subjects and the places of every eulogy are open data in any case (see
[Data and sources](/docs/data)).

## Reading two editions side by side

From any day, the reader can set another edition beside the one you are reading, eulogy by eulogy.
Eulogies are matched by their identifiers, so the same saint stands on the same line even where the two
editions number or order the day differently.
