# Names and places

With **Names & places** switched on, the reader marks the persons and the places a eulogy names, in the
2004 Latin and Italian editions: persons in amber, places in blue. Hovering over a mark, or focusing it
with the keyboard, opens a card about it. This page explains what is marked, how a name is found in the
text, which marks belong together, and how a mark is linked to Wikidata.

## Who counts as a person

Each eulogy's persons are listed once, from the Latin edition: the saints and blessed it commemorates,
in its text and in its footnotes (the lists of companions). Every person is known by their **Latin name**,
in the nominative, whatever the case the text prints it in and whatever the edition.

When a eulogy names **two persons of the same name**, they are numbered: the second is "the 2nd of this
name in this eulogy", and so on. The martyrs of Lyons on
[2 June](/read/martyrologium_romanum_2004/06/02#mr:0602-pothinus-et-socii), for example, include two
women named Pompeia, and the list says so: the second is "another Pompeia". The number is part of who a
person is, so the two are always two persons, never one.

How the list of persons tells a second person from the same person named again:

- in a **list of companions**, a name printed again is another person of that name;
- **in the text**, a name printed again is the same person, unless the text says it is another
  (*alius*, *alter*, *altera* in Latin);
- a name printed **in pieces**, with another name of the person in between (*cognomento*, *seu*, *qui
  et*), is one person, and so is a name with a parenthesis inside it (a religious name followed by the
  secular name and the surname).

## How a name is found in the text

A person's mark is placed where the edition prints their name, whatever its form:

- **in Latin**, the name in any of its cases (*Pauli*, *Paulo*, *Paulum* for Paulus), with its particles
  (*a Sancto Ioseph*, *de*), and with the other names the text gives the person;
- **in Italian**, the Italian form of the name: the one the eulogy's subject gives in Italian, or the Latin name with
  its given names translated (Ioannes becomes Giovanni) and the surname kept; failing that, the surname
  alone, widened to the whole name printed around it;
- in a list, the persons are printed **in the same order** in both editions, so each is looked for only
  between their neighbours in the list;
- where the edition **misprints** a name, and the misprint is recorded (see
  [Notes, misprints and errata](/docs/notes-and-marks)), the name is found as printed.

A mark the matcher is not sure of is never placed on its own: it is proposed to the curators, who accept
it, correct it or reject it.

## Which marks are tinted together

Hovering over a person tints **every mark of that person in the eulogy**: in its text, in its footnotes,
and, when two editions are compared side by side, in both columns. Marks of different persons are never
tinted together, even when they share a name: the two Pompeias above are tinted apart. A place's mark is
tinted alone.

What makes two marks the same person is the person's Latin name and number in the eulogy, not their
Wikidata item: most of the persons a eulogy names have no item at all.

## Wikidata

A person is linked to a Wikidata item only when exactly one item passes every check: a human being, with
the status of a saint or a blessed, under a matching name, and, where the eulogy marks the day of death,
dead on that day. A one-word name in a list of companions is linked only when the item died on the
eulogy's very day: a famous namesake is likelier than the obscure martyr. Every other case is decided by
the curators, and a person with no item has a mark with no link.

A place is linked to its Wikidata item through the site's **gazetteer**, with its modern country, which
places it on the [map](/map); its role in the eulogy (place of death, see, burial…) is shown on its card.
See [Data and sources](/docs/data) for where all of this is kept, and
[Contributing](/docs/contributing) to report a wrong mark or a wrong item.
